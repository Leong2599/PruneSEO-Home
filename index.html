Approach

Built around evidence, not SEO guesswork

There is a lot of noise in this market. These are the five commitments we actually build to.

Our crawler builds the inventory

The inventory comes from our crawler, so the starting point is the URLs we can actually reach rather than whatever a search engine has already indexed.

Observed is kept separate from inferred

A status code, title, canonical or robots directive we fetched is observed. A conclusion drawn from URL relationships or groups is inferred. The report labels both, so a diagnosis cannot quietly pass as a fact.

Patterns are structural

URLs are grouped by the shape of their path, parameters and signals, so a finding covers a whole template rather than the rows you happened to look at.

Inferred labels say so

Where a conclusion is our inference from crawl signals rather than a fact we fetched, the interface labels it as inferred. Duplicate groups are the clearest case: the group is derived by comparing canonical declarations, so it is marked inferred rather than presented as observed.

Progress is measured, not asserted

A re-crawl re-reads the same patterns so you can tell which change moved the number and which one changed nothing. Every plan includes four crawls per website per month.

Crawler-first Observed vs inferred Pattern clustering Evidence-based Large-site ready

Two caveats we would rather state than bury. PruneSEO tells you what is wrong with your indexation and what to change; it does not guarantee that Google indexes anything, and it does not promise rankings. And the demo above is illustrative: the real product reports how many URLs it fetched and which ones it could not, rather than treating silence as a clean result.
